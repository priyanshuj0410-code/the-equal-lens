# Funder map

Related: [case for support](case-for-support.md) · [readiness](readiness.md) · [pipeline](pipeline.md)

The short version: for the next year, our money comes from Indian individuals, Indian foundations and our own donate page. CSR and government money open slowly. Foreign money stays closed until FCRA, which needs three years.

## At a glance

| Funder type | Open to us now? | What opens it | Priority |
|---|---|---|---|
| Individual philanthropists and family offices | Yes | Nothing. 80G helps some donors | 1 |
| Donation and grant platforms | Crowdfunding yes. Curated platforms mostly after 80G | 12A and 80G | 2 |
| Private and philanthropic foundations (Indian) | A few. Most want 12A, 80G and audited accounts | 12A, 80G, first audit | 3 |
| Gender, women and girls grantmakers | Indian ones, as foundations. International ones, no | 12A and 80G. FCRA for international | 4 |
| Government | For access (permissions, MoUs), yes. For grants, rarely at our stage | NGO Darpan ID, then track record | 5 |
| Corporate CSR | Not directly. Indirect routes below | 12A, 80G, CSR-1 and three audited years | 6: relationships now |
| International foundations and grantmakers | No | FCRA | 7: relationships only |

## 1. Individual philanthropists and family offices

**Why first.** The only funders who can say yes on trust alone, and the only ones fully open to us today.

**Who.** People the founders can ask directly, then the people they introduce us to. Giving circles. Family offices whose principals care about children or gender.

**What they need.** A specific ask in units ("this takes one class through Discovery"), the founders in person, and updates that show the money at work without showing children's faces.

**On 80G.** It matters only to donors who file under the old tax regime. Donors on the new regime, which is the default, get no deduction under s.133 of the Income-tax Act 2025. Mention it, but don't lead with it.

**Watch out.** Record every donor's identity. Once 80G arrives, their PAN and address go in the yearly statement. Take nothing from anyone who is not an Indian citizen, including relatives abroad on foreign passports, until we have FCRA.

## 2. Donation and grant platforms

Platform by platform, with fees, sign-up documents and the Daan Utsav plan: [platforms.md](platforms.md).

**Our own donate page first.** It is the cheapest channel, and every other appeal points to it. Until the payment gateway is live, a UPI ID and bank details with a "request a receipt" email let people give today.

**Crowdfunding** (for example Ketto or Milaap). Open now. Works best as a time-bound campaign with a concrete target, such as taking Discovery to three classes at a named partner. Tax receipts only once 80G arrives.

**Curated giving platforms** (for example GiveIndia). They run their own due diligence and generally expect 12A, 80G and financial statements. Check each platform's current NGO criteria before applying.

**Giving moments.** Daan Utsav, 2 to 8 October every year. GivingTuesday, 1 December 2026. January to March, when old-regime taxpayers make tax-saving donations.

**Watch out.** Any platform that accepts international cards on our behalf puts us in breach of FCRA. Turn international payments off.

## 3. Private and philanthropic foundations (Indian)

**What they fund.** Programmes with a clear theory of change, a costed budget and a measurement plan. Some run small grants for young organisations.

**What they need.** 12A and 80G, audited accounts (often two or three years), budget, case, measurement plan, and governance and safeguarding policies. See [readiness](readiness.md).

**Route.** Open calls, and warm introductions from people who already know us.

**Watch out.** Some Indian foundations hold foreign money. Since 2020 they cannot pass foreign contribution on to us, even though they are registered in India. Ask whether a grant would come from domestic funds.

## 4. Gender, women and girls grantmakers

**Fit.** Strong for funds focused on gender norms, violence prevention or adolescents. Two things these funds often ask about: whether we are women-led (three women co-founders are listed on our site, and the founders decide how we describe ourselves) and whether we work with boys (we do, in every programme).

**Check each fund's focus.** Some fund only work led by or directed at women and girls. Our mixed-gender, norms-change work is a better fit for some funds than others.

**Route.** Treat Indian ones like foundations (section 3). Most of the large international ones need FCRA (section 7). Map them now so we are ready in year three.

## 5. Government

**Money.** Cash grants to organisations our age are rare and slow. Don't plan the year around them.

**Access is the early win.** Permission from a district education office to run sessions in government schools, or from the District Child Protection Unit for children's homes, builds the track record every other funder asks for.

**Hooks.** The School Health and Wellness Programme themes (see the [coverage map](case-for-support.md#where-we-fit)) and the life-skills emphasis in NEP 2020.

**What it needs.** An NGO Darpan ID, which is free. Register now.

## 6. Corporate CSR

**The rule.** Under Rule 4(1) of the Companies (CSR Policy) Rules, as amended in 2021, an independent NGO must hold 12A, 80G and CSR-1 registration and have "an established track record of at least three years in undertaking similar activities". Companies usually check this against three years of audited accounts.

**So direct CSR grants are closed until our third audited year.** File CSR-1 as soon as 12A and 80G are granted anyway. Some CSR teams ask for the number before a first meeting.

**Routes open now.**

- **Service provider to a company that runs its own CSR project.** The rules let companies implement CSR directly and buy in delivery. Ask each CSR head whether they work this way. Fees count toward limits on commercial income for some charitable objects, so check with our CA before pricing.
- **Programme partner inside an established NGO's CSR grant,** paid by that NGO for delivery. Have both CAs check the structure.
- **Employee sessions paid from HR or learning budgets,** not CSR. Our adult workshops fit here.
- **Employees as individual donors.** Treat as section 1.

**Volunteering asks.** Corporate volunteers default to no child contact: materials prep, translation, skills-based help. Sessions likely to lead to disclosures are closed to visitors. Don't tell a company its one-day volunteers need police verification; the host site's own visitor policy applies on its premises.

**Fit.** Schedule VII item (ii), education, especially among children. Item (iii), gender equality.

**Timing.** Most CSR budgets follow the financial year starting 1 April. Begin conversations between October and February for the following year.

**Multinationals.** CSR money from the Indian arm of a foreign company counts as domestic when foreign shareholding is within FEMA limits. Confirm company by company.

## 7. International foundations and grantmakers

**Closed until FCRA.** Any money from a foreign source needs FCRA registration or prior permission. Registration needs three years of existence and ₹15 lakh spent on core activities over the last three financial years.

**No workaround through a partner.** Since the 2020 amendment, an FCRA-registered organisation cannot transfer foreign contribution to anyone else.

**Prior permission.** Possible for a specific donor, amount and purpose, backed by a commitment letter. It is slow, and worth it only for a large, committed grant.

**Now.** Build relationships and learning partnerships, and take no money. Plan the FCRA application for the year we meet both thresholds.

|---|---|
