## Title

**Play kits that teach underprivileged children gender equality**

Alternatives, same meaning:

- Help underprivileged children learn gender equality through play
- Activity kits for free gender-equality sessions in schools and children's homes

## One-line summary

Free eight-session programmes teach children aged 5 to 12 about fairness, safety and speaking up, through games. The games need materials, and that is what your donation buys.

## Story

The Equal Lens runs free programmes on gender equality and life skills for children aged 5 to 12, in schools and children's homes. Eight sessions, one a week. The programme costs the school or home nothing, and it costs the children nothing.

Each session runs 1 hour 40 minutes, and an hour of that is a game. Children are not lectured. They play, and then they talk about what happened in the game. Over the eight weeks they work out that a job is not a boy's job or a girl's job, that chores at home are everybody's, that their body belongs to them, that they are allowed to say no, and that speaking up for another child is a normal thing to do.

The games only work with materials in children's hands. Every child needs their own cards, paper and colours during the session, and something to take home at the end of it. Every new class needs a new set.

That is exactly what you are buying here. You choose the items, DonateKart delivers them to us, and they go into the next classroom. None of it pays for salaries, rent or travel. It is the material a child holds during a session.

## About us

The Equal Lens helps children unlearn bias and relearn empathy through play, before inherited ideas harden into lifelong assumptions. We run three programmes, for ages 5 to 12, 13 to 15 and 16 to 18, and they are free to every school and children's home that hosts us. A member of staff from the host is in the room for every session.

- Colour pencils, crayons and sketch pens
- A4 paper and chart paper
- Glue sticks, and scissors for facilitators to use, not children
- Card stock for certificates
- A lidded box or bag to hold one class kit
- A jar with a lid for each group, used as the Ask-It jar where children post questions
- Printed activity and mission cards, if DonateKart can supply printing

## Rules for the listing

- **No photographs of children.** Use pictures of the materials, the kits packed, illustrations, or the team at work. This holds even when a platform or a donor asks for faces.
- **"Underprivileged" is used because it is the word donors search for.** If any partner school is fee-paying, change the title and story to "the schools and children's homes we work with", because donors read it literally. "Children in low-income schools and children's homes" is the more accurate phrase if it fits the partners we actually have.
- **No numbers we cannot show.** No children reached, no class sizes, no prices, until they are recorded.
- **Tax.** Ask DonateKart who issues donors' 80G certificates. If its own foundation does, our pending 80G costs donors nothing. If it does not, say plainly that gifts are not tax-deductible yet.
- **Say nothing about behaviour change.** The programme can be described by what it teaches, not by results we have not measured.
