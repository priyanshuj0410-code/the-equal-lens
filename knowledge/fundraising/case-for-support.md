# Case for support

Related: [funder map](funder-map.md) · [budget](budget.md) · [readiness](readiness.md)

## In one paragraph

Children learn the rules about who gets to do what long before anyone invites them to question those rules. The Equal Lens runs play-based programmes that help children and teenagers notice those rules, question them and practise fairer ones, from age 5 to 18. The programmes are free to every school and children's home that hosts them. We are raising money to take them to more children.

## The problem

**Gender rules are in place by early adolescence.** The Global Early Adolescent Study (Johns Hopkins Bloomberg School of Public Health and the World Health Organization, 15 countries including India) found that the onset of adolescence brings a strikingly similar set of rigidly enforced gender expectations everywhere, and that these expectations are linked to lifelong risks including depression, violence and HIV. [1]

**Adults carry the same rules, and hand them on.** In the Pew Research Center's survey of 29,999 Indian adults (fieldwork November 2019 to March 2020, published March 2022), 87% agreed completely or mostly that "a wife must always obey her husband", and 80% agreed that when jobs are scarce, men should have more right to a job than women. Women agreed almost as often as men. [2] These are shared norms, not one group's opinion, and children absorb them from everyone around them.

**Schools are already asked to teach this.** The government's School Health and Wellness Programme lists gender equality, emotional wellbeing, interpersonal relationships, safety against violence and safe use of the internet among its 11 themes. [3] Our sessions teach several of those themes directly, so we support work the school already has to do rather than adding to its load. The [coverage map](#where-we-fit) shows which themes we cover and which we do not.

Do not use the statistics currently on the website homepage (70%, 85%, 92%). They have no source. See [readiness](readiness.md#5-what-funders-see-on-our-website).

## What we do

We help people unlearn bias and relearn empathy through play, before inherited ideas harden into lifelong assumptions.

One ladder, three programmes, no overlap:

| Programme | Ages | Sessions | The job |
|---|---|---|---|
| The Discovery Program | 5 to 12 | 8 | **Naming.** Children get the vocabulary and the rules |
| The Teen Compass | 13 to 15 | 7 | **Navigating.** Me → Us → Power |
| The Relay Program | 16 to 18 | 6 | **Crossing.** Choose → Enter → Carry, and the handover |

Every session runs 1 hr 40 min, built around a 60-minute game. Sessions run weekly, with the partner's safeguarding lead in the room throughout. Across the ladder, children cover self-worth and feelings, gender and fairness, speaking up, body safety and trusted adults, working together and online safety. Teenagers go on to relationships, consent, power, pressure and their first rights at work.

We also run workshops for adults aged 18 to 35 in workplaces, campuses and communities.

## Why this approach

1. **It starts early.** Discovery begins at 5, well before the age at which the evidence above finds the rules already set.
2. **It is a ladder, not a talk.** Up to 21 sessions between 5 and 18, each programme picking up where the last one ended. A one-off assembly is easy to forget. These programmes are built to be repeated across years.
3. **Children do, rather than listen.** Sixty of every hundred minutes are a game.
4. **Boys and girls learn together.** Everyone learns the same words for fairness, feelings and safety, and boys are part of the conversation about gender rather than the subject of it.
5. **Safeguarding is designed in.** Sessions likely to lead to a disclosure (body safety, pressure) are closed to visitors. Facilitators work to a written disclosure protocol and refer to local services rather than counsel. A peer facilitator is never the safeguarding responder.

## How we measure, and what we can claim

Our design is a short check-in before and after each programme, covering stated knowledge, attitudes and confidence.

- **What it supports:** stated knowledge, attitudes and confidence moved in the intended direction.
- **What it does not support:** any claim about behaviour. There is no comparison group, so we cannot attribute change to the programme alone.
- **How children's data is held:** pseudonymous codes, with the only child-to-code list held by the partner's safeguarding lead, never by us. Guardian consent under the DPDP Act 2023, s.9. No tracking or behavioural monitoring. The check-in is stated to children as not a way to report anything about themselves.

The programmes page already promises these check-ins. Before quoting any result, confirm they ran and that the data is held as above.

## What we will not do

- **No photographs of children**, including for a funder's report. We report with materials, the team at work, approved illustrations and real numbers.
- **No clinical content.** No contraception, STIs or mental-health treatment. We teach body literacy, consent, gender norms and emotional accountability, and refer the rest to local services.
- **No claims we cannot defend.**

Funders sometimes ask for the first two. Saying no, with the reason, is part of the case.

## Limitations

- **We are young.** 12A and 80G are in process. We are not yet eligible for direct CSR grants, which need three audited years, or for foreign money, which needs FCRA. See [funder map](funder-map.md).
- **Our evidence will be single-group pre/post.** It shows change in what children say, not in what they do.
- **We are a small founding team.** Growth depends on training facilitators, and each one needs verification and training before working with children.
- **Delivery follows partners' calendars.** Exams and board years limit what older groups can finish, which is why Relay is six sessions.

## The ask

Asks are made in units from [budget.md](budget.md): one child through a programme, one class through a programme, one partner for a year. The figures go here once the budget inputs are in.

Where a funder allows it, ask for unrestricted money first. It pays for training, verification, audit and measurement, which no project grant covers.
